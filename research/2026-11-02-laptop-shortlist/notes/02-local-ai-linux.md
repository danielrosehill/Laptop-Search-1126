# Compute for local AI on Linux: CPUs, iGPUs, NPUs, and Ubuntu/KDE hardware support

## Answer
For the buyer's main load (Paraformer on CPU, 4-8 terminal sessions), any current 8-12 core x86 laptop CPU is enough. Paraformer int8 in sherpa-onnx runs at a real-time factor of about 0.08-0.2 on 1-2 CPU threads [S0210], so the NPU is not needed for ASR and no NPU route for Paraformer was found. For a local agent LLM, memory bandwidth and RAM size decide the result, not NPU TOPS. Strix Halo (Ryzen AI Max, 256-bit memory, up to 128 GB) is the only laptop class here that runs 30B+ models comfortably; it is roughly 2x faster than Strix Point on the same 7B Vulkan test [S0209]. Strix Point/Gorgon Point and Intel Panther Lake sit at about 120-150 GB/s and suit 7-14B models at moderate speed. NPUs are usable on Linux but narrow. AMD's XDNA2 works through FastFlowLM/Lemonade for small LLMs and through ONNX Runtime with the Vitis AI EP on Ubuntu 24.04 only [S0206][S0208]. Intel's NPU works through OpenVINO on Ubuntu 24.04 and 26.04 [S0205]. Snapdragon X2 is not ready: on Ubuntu Wi-Fi, audio and webcam do not work out of the box, and Canonical certification is not expected before 2027 [S0204]. Recommendation: choose 32 GB as the floor and 64 GB if the laptop will run local LLMs. Choose Strix Halo only if 30B+ models matter, and accept a heavier, hotter machine. Otherwise choose Strix Point/Gorgon Point or Panther Lake and verify the WWAN module and eSIM per model before buying.

## Evidence
- The amdxdna NPU driver is in the mainline kernel. Gorgon Halo needs only a two-line device-ID patch (Linux 7.4 target), because its NPU is effectively the same as in Strix Point, Strix Halo and Gorgon Point. Phoronix says Linux NPU support is "still not as mature" as Windows. [S0201]
- AMD's Ryzen AI software on Linux supports Ubuntu 24.04 with kernel 6.10+ (not 26.04), Python 3.12, Strix Point and newer. Only the NPU flow exists on Linux, with no iGPU path. It supports INT8/BF16 CNNs, BF16 encoder models and NPU-only LLMs, and recommends 64 GB RAM. [S0208]
- FastFlowLM 1.0 (August 2026, part of AMD's ROCm family) runs LLMs and VLMs on the XDNA2 NPU under Ubuntu 24.04/25.10/26.04 and Debian 13. Linux support began in March 2026 via Lemonade. The model examples are small (Llama 3.2 1B, Qwen3-4B). The article gives no throughput figures and does not mention ASR. [S0206]
- Intel NPU driver 1.38.0 is verified on Meteor, Arrow, Lunar, Panther and Wildcat Lake, on Ubuntu 24.04 and 26.04, with OpenVINO 2026.3.1. [S0205] NPU 5 is quoted at 50 TOPS INT8 (spec-listing summary, not opened). [S0215]
- Paraformer int8 CPU RTF: 0.073-0.079 (1 thread, zh-int8-2025-10-07), 0.15 (2 threads, trilingual), 0.195 (2 threads, English), on unnamed hardware. Models are 79-234 MB, so memory bandwidth and RAM barely matter for ASR. [S0210]
- llama.cpp Vulkan, Llama 2 7B Q4_0, community table: "Ryzen AI Max+ 300" pp512 1487-1704 and tg128 51.8-54.4 t/s, against "Ryzen AI 300" pp512 479-533 and tg128 22.4-23.9 t/s. The 2x text-generation gap fits the memory-bandwidth difference. Rows are undated and the configurations are not stated. [S0209]
- Phoronix, Panther Lake X7 358H (Arc B390) vs Ryzen AI 9 HX 370: Panther Lake wins Vulkan prompt processing, and HX 370 wins text generation on Mistral 7B and Granite 3B. [S0203] On CPU, Panther Lake is strong in text generation but AVX-512 AMD laptops lead; OpenVINO also favours AVX-512 AMD. [S0211]
- Strix Halo on Ubuntu 26.04: about 20% geo-mean gain in Vulkan (RADV) llama.cpp over Ubuntu 25.04. ROCm was not yet officially supported on 26.04 in April 2026. [S0202]
- Gorgon Point (Ryzen AI 9 HX 470, Radeon 890M, 12C/24T): Ubuntu 26.04 matches CachyOS within about 1% and Linux Vulkan llama.cpp is often faster than Windows. This is a mini PC, but it shows the platform is mainstream on Linux. [S0212]
- Strix Halo has 128 GB unified memory with up to 112 GB GPU-allocatable on Windows, per AMD [S0214]. The Linux-side limit (roughly 96 GB via GTT) comes from a secondary article seen in search results and was not opened or confirmed.
- Intel Panther Lake X9 388H: LPDDR5X-9600, 128-bit, about 153.6 GB/s (arithmetic checks), up to 96 GB [S0215, unverified by reading]. Kernel gains continue through 7.1, 7.2 and 7.3, and 26.04 ships 7.0, so a newer kernel helps [S0213].
- Memory bandwidth drives token generation: AMD states LLM speed is memory-speed sensitive and larger models are bandwidth-bound. [S0216, summary only] Rule of thumb (inference): decode tokens/s is at most bandwidth divided by model bytes. A 7B Q4 model (about 4 GB) at 120 GB/s gives at most about 30 t/s; a 30B Q4 model (about 17 GB) gives about 7 t/s on Strix Point or Panther Lake, and about 12-13 t/s on Strix Halo at 256 GB/s. These figures are my arithmetic, not measured. MoE models run faster since only active experts are read.
- RAM sizing (inference): a 30B Q4 model takes about 17-20 GB, plus KV cache. 32 GB leaves little room beside 8 agent sessions, a browser and a VM. 64 GB is the practical size for 14-30B models alongside the normal workload.
- Snapdragon X2 Elite Extreme on Ubuntu 26.10 concept ISO (Linux 7.3): graphics work, Wi-Fi, audio and webcam do not, some benchmarks failed, and no power telemetry exists. [S0204] Secondary search results say X2 Ubuntu certification is targeted for H1 2027 and the mainline device trees arrive in Linux 7.4. These were not opened. Verdict: not for this purchase.
- WWAN on Linux: Lenovo's enablement is per module and per system, and unlisted systems are unsupported. Quectel modules need kernel 6.5/6.6+ and ModemManager 1.22+. It is tested on Ubuntu 24.04 and Fedora, and blocked for US SIMs. [S0207] The README does not mention eSIM. Forum reports say eSIM via LPAC downloads a profile, but ModemManager integration is rough, and Fibocom FM350 has enable/disable loop reports. [S0217, summary only] An integrated eSIM that works well on Linux is therefore not assured. Test with a physical SIM first, and check the module against [S0207].
- Platform Linux health: Panther Lake in the Framework Laptop 13 Pro ran Ubuntu 26.04 out of the box with display, graphics and haptic touchpad working (from a search summary of Phoronix's review, not opened). [S0213] covers the same machine's kernel scaling. Gorgon Point works fine on 26.04 [S0212].

## Disagreements and gaps
- No measured Paraformer/sherpa-onnx RTF per CPU found. S0210 does not name its hardware, so the CPU ranking for ASR is inferred (Zen 5 and Panther Lake are both fast enough). No source shows Paraformer on any NPU, and ONNX Runtime NPU paths need model-specific conversion (inferred).
- Phoronix charts (S0203, S0211) were not readable as text, so numbers for Panther Lake vs Strix Point llama.cpp are directional only. The Strix Halo vs Strix Point figures in S0209 are community rows without config or date. No Strix Halo 13-16B/30B Linux measurement was read; the 12 t/s figure is arithmetic.
- Strix Halo's 256-bit bus and about 256 GB/s were not confirmed from a source I could open (AMD page timed out or 404). A llama.cpp discussion reportedly measured about 230 GB/s on Ubuntu 24.04 (search summary only).
- Not covered with sources: Wi-Fi 7 chip behaviour (Intel BE200/BE201, MediaTek MT7925, Qualcomm) on Ubuntu 24.04/26.04, suspend/s2idle on specific models, MIPI/IPU webcam drivers (Intel IPU7 on Panther Lake), fingerprint (libfprint) support, and KDE specifics. These are per-model and belong to the shortlist agent. Intel's IPU webcam path is often the weak spot, which is my assumption, not confirmed here.
- Ryzen AI 400 laptop SKUs vary: Ryzen 5 439/7 449 reportedly have no NPU (search summaries only). Ryzen AI Max 400 "Gorgon Halo" laptops were "nearing the marketplace" on 2026-09-30, so availability and pricing are unclear [S0201].
- Ubuntu 26.04 vs 24.04 trade-off: AMD's Ryzen AI software officially covers 24.04 only [S0208], whereas Intel's NPU driver covers both [S0205]. For an Ubuntu 26.04 install, AMD NPU use means workarounds. This matters little if the NPU is unused.

## Confidence
Medium. The NPU status, the Linux status of Snapdragon X2 and the Paraformer CPU speed rest on primary or Phoronix pages that I read. The bandwidth-to-LLM-speed link and the 32 vs 64 GB recommendation are standard reasoning, partly arithmetic. Hardware-compatibility details (Wi-Fi 7, webcam, fingerprint, eSIM) are thinly sourced, and Intel Panther Lake specs came from search summaries only.
